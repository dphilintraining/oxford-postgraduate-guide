Finding your place at Oxford.

Understand the collegiate system and explore what matters when choosing a college as a postgraduate student.

College life

Your department and college play different roles.

At Oxford, the academic department or faculty manages your postgraduate course and research supervision, while your college provides a separate community with its own people, facilities, and support.

The balance varies depending on your course, college, and personal priorities. A good way to evaluate colleges is to consider how your everyday experience might differ.

What should you compare?

01

Accommodation

Availability, location, contract length, affordability, and access requirements.

02

Community

Graduate student spaces, events, and opportunities to meet people beyond your department.

03

Practical facilities

Libraries, dining, accessibility, sports, support services, and the commute to your department.

04

Scholarships

College-specific awards and whether a stated college preference is required for consideration.

Does college choice affect admission?

According to Oxford, your college preference does not affect the academic department's admission decision. For matriculated courses, candidates who receive a departmental offer are guaranteed a college place, though it may not be at their preferred college.

Don't over-optimise for prestige. When comparing colleges, consider tangible factors such as housing, affordability, access needs, graduate community, and where you will actually spend your time.
