Applying to Oxford, step by step.

A friendly starting point for understanding Oxford’s postgraduate applications, whether you are considering a taught master’s, research master’s, or DPhil.

Application essentials

Start with your course, not the application form.

Oxford has a wide range of postgraduate courses, and each course page defines its own requirements. Before drafting essays, identify the programme that genuinely matches your academic interests and look at its admissions criteria, supporting documents, deadlines, and funding information.

Identify the exact application route, deadline, and document requirements for that course.

Build your materials

Prepare references, transcripts, and any statement, CV, proposal, or written work requested.

04

Review and submit

Give yourself time to proofread, verify details, and complete the application in advance.

Course-specific requirements win. Do not assume that an application checklist for one MSc or DPhil applies to another. Oxford’s official course pages and Graduate Application Guide are the authoritative sources.

Master's versus DPhil: what's different?

Taught master’s courses and doctoral programmes can differ significantly in how they select applicants and evaluate research readiness. Some DPhil routes are built around a proposed research project or identified supervisor; other structured programmes recruit students into a broader training pathway.

That is why a strong application starts with understanding the specific course, rather than applying a universal formula.

Should you contact a potential supervisor?

It depends on the course. Some research programmes expect applicants to identify or approach prospective supervisors in advance, while others do not. Read your chosen course's instructions and Oxford's research-project and supervisor guidance before reaching out.

If contact is encouraged, a short, well-researched message explaining your interests and fit is a sensible first step. Do not interpret a lack of response as an admissions decision.

A sample first email to a potential supervisor

This is an original illustrative template, not a message to copy verbatim. Adapt it only if your course encourages prospective supervisor contact.

Subject: Prospective DPhil applicant interested in [research topic]

Dear Professor [Surname],

I am [your position] at [institution], working on [brief research area]. Your recent research on [specific finding] prompted my interest because [personal reason].

I am exploring Oxford's [course name] for 2027 entry and wondered whether you expect to consider new DPhil students in this area. I would welcome the opportunity to learn whether there might be a research fit. My CV is attached for context.

Thank you for your time.
Best wishes,
[Your name]

Your application must be your own work

Oxford requires application statements, research proposals and supporting writing to reflect the applicant's own ideas and expression. Consult the University's guidance on personal statements and AI use in graduate applications. An example is not a template to submit.

A practical preparation timeline

Rather than treating these dates as fixed, work backwards from your course’s published deadline:

  1. Several months out: explore courses, projects, supervisors, and funding routes.
  2. Before the deadline: ask referees early, gather transcripts, and draft required documents.
  3. Final weeks: check word limits, supporting documents, reference status, and scholarship requirements.
  4. After submission: follow official communications and prepare for a possible interview where applicable.

A few common questions

Do I need publications to apply for a DPhil?

Publications are not a universal requirement. Admissions criteria vary by course; focus on evidence of research preparation, academic achievement, and fit with your intended programme.

Do I need a research proposal?

Only if your course requires one. Some programmes request detailed project plans while others use different application materials. Always use the document specifications on your course page.

Are application deadlines the same for every course?

No. Deadlines and scholarship consideration dates differ. Check the relevant course page for the current cycle rather than relying on a generic annual deadline.
